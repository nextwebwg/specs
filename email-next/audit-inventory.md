# Standards audit inventory

This working inventory records source retrieval and reading separately. It is not a completed compliance assessment. Source snapshots and review cutoffs are recorded in the individual audits.

> [!warn] Coverage remains open
> Downloading a document, collecting its headings, or finding an update does not mean it has been read or audited. Every RFC in the current inventory has a recorded base-text review and section dispositions. Errata freshness, applicable normative dependencies, registries and implementation coverage remain separate obligations; their status is recorded in each audit. A completed base-text review does not close those obligations. The [review index](audits/index.md) links all 159 recorded base-document reviews and lists missing update/replacement targets. The inventory expands as dependent specifications and registries are examined.

Current corpus: **159 RFC records**. Captured full-corpus errata for these RFCs: **536 records** (Held for Document Update: 125, Rejected: 108, Reported: 104, Verified: 199). These counts describe retrieved evidence, not review completion.

The [machine-readable inventory](audits/source-inventory.json) records SHA-256 digests and byte lengths for all 159 retained RFC base-text snapshots and their 159 metadata snapshots. A digest identifies the captured bytes, not their interpretation, freshness or authenticity.

## Remaining scope discovery

The initial set does not close the email standards corpus. Still to enumerate and classify are remaining transitive updates, the normative dependency graph, SMTP and IMAP extensions, Sieve extensions, POP extensions, DNS/service discovery, calendar scheduling over mail, cryptographic discovery records, MIME registrations, and current emailcore revisions. General-purpose dependencies need explicit boundaries. The [grammar notation audit](audits/grammar-notation.md) covers ABNF and its case-sensitive literal update; selected TLS and Unicode reviews are linked from the [audit index](audits/index.md). These reviews do not establish closure of the remaining dependency graph.

RFC text and relationship metadata were retrieved from the RFC Editor. Errata came from its [errata dataset](https://www.rfc-editor.org/errata.json). ## Audit procedure

For each source, record publication status, the version consulted, updates and replacements, relevant errata and their status, and every numbered section. Review appendices and grammar as well as prose. For each point, record inherited behavior, extension opportunity, conflict, non-applicability, or an unresolved decision, with a link to the affected proposal section.

Verified errata, reported errata, rejected errata, and items held for a document update are different evidence states. A downloaded erratum is not automatically a corrected requirement. Internet-Drafts and queued revisions likewise do not silently replace a published RFC.

The audit must cover architecture; SMTP transport and submission; message grammar and internationalization; MIME and rendering; delivery reports and automation; mailbox access and synchronization; authentication and transport security; end-to-end protection; lists and forwarding; and extension registries. References discovered during review expand the inventory or receive an explicit scope rationale. The inventory is not closed simply because a convenient number of documents has been retrieved.

Completion requires a disposition for every point in the established corpus, reviewed update/errata relationships, and resolved conflicts or explicitly retained proposal questions. Implementation tests are separate evidence: specification compatibility alone does not prove interoperability.
