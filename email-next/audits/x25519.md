# X25519 key agreement

[RFC7748](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7748.html) defines X25519, the Diffie–Hellman function in the [selected HPKE and MLS suite](../security-profile.md). Its byte encoding, scalar processing and input handling remain primitive requirements. It does not authenticate an email account or authorize a group participant. X448 and the Edwards mappings were reviewed without adding them to the selected suite or making signing and encryption keys interchangeable.

## Consequences for implementations

X25519 uses 32-byte strings and little-endian encoding. It masks the high bit of the received u-coordinate and accepts noncanonical field values as though reduced modulo the field prime. Scalar decoding clears the specified low and high bits and sets the required high bit. Output encoding has a zero unused high bit. These primitive rules are separate from the canonical base64 and exact-byte rules for Email Next's enclosing records.

RFC7748 permits checking for an all-zero shared secret. The selected HPKE construction is stricter: [RFC9180 Section 7.1.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9180.html#section-7.1.4) requires recipients to check and abort on an all-zero Diffie–Hellman result. The base document's MAY must not weaken that requirement.

A shared secret alone does not prove ownership of a public-key identity. Section 7 describes equivalent public keys and warns against assuming that both parties' secrets contributed to every result. Preserve HPKE's public-key/context binding and the separate accepted identity checks.

The ladder's fixed sequence of field operations does not establish constant-time behavior of an implementation. Secret-dependent memory access, branches and arithmetic timing remain concerns. Both curves are vulnerable to sufficiently capable quantum computers; this baseline is not post-quantum.

## Coverage and corrections

The complete 22-page source was read: both curves' parameters and maps, encoding and ladder pseudocode, direct and iterative vectors, both complete Diffie–Hellman examples, every reference, deterministic generation criteria and Sage scripts, acknowledgements and author addresses. Exact ranges appear in the [section ledger](section-ledger.json). No vector, million-iteration case or curve-generation script was executed in this review.

The [live errata listing](https://errata.rfc-editor.org/search/?rfc_number=7748) matches nine captured records:

## Evidence and limits

Captured metadata identifies Informational status, January 2016 publication and no listed updates or successors. The 1,235-line source SHA-256 is `279ca0ecc5e92e2962e27b846986aeb74729d9dd34bd4a04a362f80dcb596ad3`. Snapshot metadata is in the [source inventory](source-inventory.json).

This base review does not certify the library, entropy source, side-channel resistance or end-to-end key exchange. Informative mathematical and historical references are not automatically selected protocol dependencies. The HPKE and MLS constructions, identity binding, key lifecycle and implementation checks retain their own coverage obligations.
