# Design resolutions and outstanding work

This is the canonical resolution inventory for the Email/2 discussion draft. A resolved design names the contract that determines behavior, including unsupported and failure outcomes. It does not mean that an implementation exists, that tests passed or that an external organization accepted the design.

The inventory starts with the complete-client contract, encryption journey and existing interoperability coverage, then follows their referenced contracts. It is a working audit: the rows below do not establish that every chapter and source dependency has been fully re-audited. New gaps found during that review must be added rather than hidden by a completion label.

## Specification decisions

| ID | Question | Current disposition and authoritative next evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-01 | Which exact protocols and operations form one required bundle? | Defined by [email2-conversation-v0](suite-profile.md); the protected core, complete optional interaction blocks and shared signed application selection combine quorum ordering, edits and indexed replies. Joined implementation remains absent. |
| D-02 | How is indexed-reply support negotiated and carried inside encryption? | Defined by [context8, assertion8 and mime-suite-v0](suite-profile.md). Admission, ordering-wrapper versions, presentation implications, dispatch and fresh-conversation migration are specified. Positive and negative receiving tests remain required. |
| D-03 | Is HTML/CSS optional, and what happens to text? | Resolved by [required presentation](presentation-profile.md#required-presentation): both suite rendering profiles are required; text supports compatibility, matching and explicit reading views, not a text-only client conformance class. |
| D-04 | How are authored and quoted chunks located and reassembled? | Defined by [reply content](reply-content.md): opaque IDs, explicit roles, literal element/index agreement, exact source selectors, independent validation, graph/cycle rules and reversible legacy fallback. A production parser/renderer and integrated validation remain absent. |
| D-05 | How does a new person start without key administration? | [Automated first-contact setup](first-contact-setup.md) selects a content-free provider handshake, automatic enabled-client response or valid published material, then the first encrypted human message. Ordinary introductions remain a compatibility option, not a prerequisite. Explicit first-contact policy and continuity remain required. The [bootstrap contract](bootstrap.md) defines the public exchange; complete joined acceptance and independent ownership mechanisms remain unfinished; see D-23. |
| D-06 | How does a provider advertise and authorize its public control service? | Defined for known pairs by [domain delegation/native registration](provider-discovery.md) and [account/device links, approved pair grants and role-credential issuance/revocation](provider-grants.md). Sixteen discovery cases and fifteen actual identity/signature proof cases exist; complete account-login/grant/queue integration is absent. [Unknown-peer bootstrap](bootstrap.md) defines distinct source-account and recipient authority with43 component tests; full joined acceptance remains D-23. No public deployment is claimed. |
| D-07 | Can internationalized addresses enter automatic discovery? | Defined by [mailbox addresses](mailbox-addresses.md): internationalized/quoted local parts, minimal equivalent quoting, exact Unicode/case preservation, Unicode17 IDNA, equality, account/domain binding, display safety and SMTPUTF8 versus ASCII relay carriage. Thirteen address cases plus updated metadata/bootstrap cases exercise syntax and current component integration; complete assertion8/header/account/admission/SMTP/display acceptance remains required. Earlier encodings retain their historical narrower experiments. |
| D-08 | How are missing/stale revocations and freshness sources handled across providers? | Defined by [freshness, clocks and revocation knowledge](freshness.md): cached asynchronous default, explicit age of the accepted state’s declared issuance, exact live device evidence, per-device policy, credible-clock checks and serialized release dependencies. Sixteen local cases use accepted identities, actual assertion8/bootstrap signatures and durable attempt retry/cancel/reopen. Known-pair current-suite, account/association/verification and full release integration remain required; no global latest-state or independent signing-time guarantee is claimed. |
| D-09 | How does a person recover their correspondence after total device loss? | Defined by [encrypted correspondence archives](encrypted-archives.md): portable signed container, independent recovery kit, included/omitted resources, private staging, publication/retry, history-only restore and explicit rollback limits. Local container/age evidence exists; [signed collection catalogs](archive-sets.md) now specify multi-artifact completeness and atomic historical acceptance, with a local multi-file experiment. Whole-application snapshot, rich-history restore, source acquisition and managed-service integration still need evidence. |
| D-10 | Can emergency recovery preserve identity continuity? | Resolved for the initial suite: surviving-device enrollment preserves continuity; after all authorized devices are lost, use an explicitly new identity. [Archive recovery](encrypted-archives.md) restores reading, never live keys or authority. The recover role does not enable an emergency override. Any future continuity-preserving extension needs a separate authority construction and cannot be advertised by this suite. |
| D-11 | How does a new device receive authorized old history? | Defined by [authorized history transfer](history-transfer.md): exact signed request/grant/result/cancel records, recipient-specific fresh collection, explicit scope authorization, current authority and ancestry, safe expiration/retry and history-only import. Twenty-two local control/import tests include real identity proofs and revocation. Persistent journals, mail/storage handoff and multi-client integration remain implementation evidence gaps. |
| D-12 | What happens on concurrent identity branches, retirement or lost administration? | Ordinary fork/hold and explicit new-identity paths exist. Concurrent valid administrator updates or checkpoints can currently create a permanent authorized conflict despite surviving healthy devices. Define authenticated coordination/repair preserving revocations and explicit account retirement; local compare-and-swap and choosing the first/higher revision cannot resolve the distributed race. |
| D-13 | Can group administrator authority, application parameters or ownership change? | Current suite preserves explicit unsupported outcomes. Review lifecycle requirements and define any necessary authority-transfer/closure transitions rather than treating an unsupported implementation as a successful transition. |
| D-14 | How can ordering continue when selected peers are unavailable or revoked? | Existing quorum proofs, coordinator succession and atomic revoked-intermediate catch-up define safety and limited progress. A validated nonempty membership candidate can hold application sending permanently without its required quorum; two-principal/two-witness conversations require both at fault bound0. Define a small-conversation availability policy and safe distinction between pending additions and revocations, including prepared-but-revoked locks, permanent witness loss and restart. A separately admitted new conversation is currently the escape, not transparent continuation. |
| D-15 | Which hosted fallback is actually interoperable? | Bearer access has a contract and local evidence. Public management authorization, production browser trust and account-bound hosting need complete selection or an explicit unsupported boundary. |
| D-16 | Which service actions and authorization paths are in the initial extension? | Exact selected local service-answer/account-linking contracts exist. Audit complete public client acquisition, credential lifecycle, reauthorization, durable effects and compatibility; arbitrary forms remain deliberately deferred. |
| D-17 | How are per-recipient exposure, private copies and protected labels joined? | Envelope4 incorporates the protected subject/role, required personal-address, delivery and private-copy contracts in the suite. Verify no cross-product of incompatible versions or private-recipient leakage is implied. |
| D-18 | What are complete renderer semantics and processing bounds? | Finite profiles and isolated adapters exist. Audit any unspecified behavior separately from implementation gaps, especially malformed markup, resource exhaustion, data-version handling, image interpretation and accessibility overrides. |
| D-19 | What does a mailbox adapter have to preserve and synchronize? | Existing [mailbox integration](mailbox-integration.md) and transport contracts need review against the complete-client journey: acquisition, restart, duplicate identity, missing control retrieval and account changes. |
| D-20 | What receipts, presence and deletion effects exist? | No implicit read/presence protocol or remote erasure is supplied. Audit every UI outcome for explicit evidence requirements and terminal states; opening/reconstruction must not cause effects. |
| D-21 | How are registrable names and versioned extension points chosen? | Candidate event registration exists; enumerate every new media type, MLS extension and capability before submission. Draft names remain experimental; actual registry approval is external. |
| D-22 | What proves whole-client conformance rather than component support? | [Client behavior](client-behavior.md), [conformance](conformance.md) and 28 [encryption journeys](encryption-journey.md#acceptance-journeys-and-failure-cases) define targets. A suite-linked acceptance inventory must account for every required feature and dependency, with honest unsupported/not-tested outcomes. |
| D-23 | Can the first human message be encrypted without a human introduction? | Selected by [first-contact setup](first-contact-setup.md): retain all draft content, use separately authorized provider control, validate exact signed setup and MLS admission, then release the first protected message. The [bootstrap wire contract](bootstrap.md) defines exact sender-account/device authorization, provider routing, private automatic responses, publication, route follow-up and failure behavior. Forty-three component tests cover configured account/signature/control-inbox gates and offline publication allocation, including precommit child-process exit. Actual network discovery/login, complete assertion8/package/admission, relay follow-up implementation, association integration and first encrypted human-message acceptance remain required. |
| D-24 | When do temporary setup/decryption secrets disappear? | Defined by [setup-secret retirement](setup-secret-retirement.md): consumed-key deletion, cancellation/expiry, proposed600-second bootstrap request maximum, inherited package lifetime, durable retirement/restart boundary and separate archive protection. Production memory/storage/snapshot erasure assurance remains absent; logical retirement tests are not physical-erasure evidence. |
| D-25 | How do personal addresses stay encrypted while providers can still deliver? | [Private addressing through provider relays](private-routing.md) selects shared authorized outer senders, opaque recipient aliases, encrypted personal From/To/Cc and authenticated client display. [Apple relay prior art](audits/relay-aliasing.md) supplies aliasing and sender-policy examples. [The required encrypted personal-address record](private-address-presentation.md) defines encrypted personal addresses, role binding, compatible scope, negotiation and display/assurance. Fifteen component cases include actual MLS/MIME/SMTP carriage under configured context. [Relay route issuance/use/revocation](relay-routes.md) now defines exact source permissions, recipient approval, delivery proofs, account/peer blocks, local replay/acceptance, key retirement and direct independent-relay retrieval. Twenty-seven configured signature/current-identity/SQLite cases exist. Public discovery/link/issuance, all protected control-carrier paths, actual SMTP/mailbox integration and complete suite acceptance still need evidence and remaining binding audit. |
| D-26 | Can one Email/2 specification support independently adopted modules and invisible protection step-ups? | Defined by [modules](modules.md), exact [protected-core/optional-operation selection](suite-profile.md#module-agreement-and-common-operation-selection) and the [address-swap sequence](private-routing.md#when-the-addresses-change). Sender/recipient masking and verification are independent, with named capability/state indicators rather than invented numeric security levels. Eleven component tests cover all eight operation combinations and every two-device intersection; joined signed admission, provider route implementation and complete module/client acceptance remain required. Current maximal common optional-module selection is immutable and excludes a later core-only client even if the optional feature was never used; safe partial-client admission/migration and intentional initial selection need resolution. |
| D-27 | Which unchanged authority may survive an unrelated identity-head advance? | [Unchanged-binding ancestry](provider-grants.md#retain-an-unchanged-device-binding) now preserves account links, pair grants and relay endpoint permissions without rewriting proofs or extending lifetimes. Local signed-successor, fork, revocation and expiry cases exercise grants/routes. Device advertisement, bootstrap and freshness integration still need a purpose-specific audit; D-27 remains open until that wider coupling is resolved. |
| D-28 | Does QR pairing finish with usable encrypted sending? | [Send-ready pairing](identity-lifecycle.md#finish-pairing-with-usable-encrypted-sending) requires automatic account connection, confidential transfer of contact/security configuration, current public setup and automatic group admission, separately from message history. The [confidential setup binding](identity-lifecycle.md#confidential-security-configuration-transfer) defines exact request/response/receipt, encrypted contact/verification/policy content, reconciliation and provider-connection boundaries. Twelve local transfer cases and the [reference contact-store integration](evidence/README.md#paired-contact-store-installation) join actual pairing, signatures, age, live trust installation, inherited verification and scoped installed receipts. Source/target identity and policy conflicts, withdrawals, current local removals and original expiry remain effective. Portable authenticated carrier binding, provider login, automatic usable-setup acquisition, existing-group admission/scheduling and joined send-ready acceptance remain unfinished. Enrollment or a staged receipt alone is insufficient. |
| D-29 | How are key expiry and automatic renewal distinguished from a fixed rotation schedule? | [Admission lifetime and renewal](security-profile.md#experimental-admission-lifetime) define signed expiry, configurable maximum/default, fresh replacement, immutable attempts and separate device/epoch/archive lifecycles. Real-package configurable-policy and expiry evidence exists locally; complete automatic publication/binding renewal, concurrent renewal handling and joined refresh/admission acceptance remain required. No universal rotation frequency is selected. |


“Open design” is not satisfied by deleting the requirement, relabeling it external, or pointing only to an experiment. A design that intentionally rejects an operation needs a complete safe outcome and must still satisfy the intended client journey.

## Evidence and dependencies

The [interoperability table](interoperability.md#contract-coverage-and-remaining-decisions) remains the detailed implementation/evidence index. This inventory owns the question and resolution status; the linked protocol chapters own the actual normative rules. Keep both consistent when a design is resolved.

Public provider adoption, independently developed clients, independent security review, standards participation and registry approval require external evidence. They must not be marked complete from local code or examples. Conversely, lack of an external participant does not excuse an unfinished wire binding or algorithm.

Application pricing, plans, licensing and backup-service retention promises belong in private product specifications. The protocol must make archives and protected conversations interoperable without depending on a commercial subscription. No product plan grants protocol authority.
