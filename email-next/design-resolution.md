# Design resolutions and outstanding work

This is the canonical resolution inventory for the Email/2 discussion draft. A resolved design names the contract that determines behavior, including unsupported and failure outcomes. It does not mean that an implementation exists, that tests passed or that an external organization accepted the design.

The inventory starts with the complete-client contract, encryption journey and existing interoperability coverage, then follows their referenced contracts. It is a working audit: the rows below do not establish that every chapter and source dependency has been fully re-audited. New gaps found during that review must be added rather than hidden by a completion label.

“Open design” is not satisfied by deleting the requirement, relabeling it external, or pointing only to an experiment. A design that intentionally rejects an operation needs a complete safe outcome and must still satisfy the intended client journey.

## Distinguish the remaining work

A specification decision is resolved only when its owning contract gives an implementer the wire format, authority, algorithm and failure outcome needed for that behavior. A missing rule or contradiction is internal specification work. The current chapter/dependency review remains open; the presence of a resolution row is not proof that every affected clause was checked.

Implementation evidence is a separate obligation. A defined rule may still lack a complete reference implementation, current-suite acceptance or deployed adapter. Those gaps prevent the corresponding implementation/conformance claim; they do not by themselves mean that the wire rule is undefined. Conversely, a passing historical experiment cannot fill an unspecified current rule. Keep each result bound to its actual source revision and tested scope.

External outcomes include independent implementations/review, provider participation and registry or standards decisions. They require evidence from those participants. Naming an internal rule as external cannot close a missing algorithm, and an old publication or completion record cannot certify a changed draft.

## Evidence and dependencies

The [interoperability table](interoperability.md#contract-coverage-and-remaining-decisions) remains the detailed implementation/evidence index. This inventory owns the question and resolution status; the linked protocol chapters own the actual normative rules. Keep both consistent when a design is resolved.

Public provider adoption, independently developed clients, independent security review, standards participation and registry approval require external evidence. They must not be marked complete from local code or examples. Conversely, lack of an external participant does not excuse an unfinished wire binding or algorithm.

Application pricing, plans, licensing and backup-service retention promises belong in private product specifications. The protocol must make archives and protected conversations interoperable without depending on a commercial subscription. No product plan grants protocol authority.
