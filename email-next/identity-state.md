# Identity-state records

An identity state names the device bindings an account currently authorizes. A directory distributes these records; it does not acquire permission to change them. This chapter defines the retained state0 format and the selected [state1 administration lifecycle](#concurrent-administration-and-retirement), including repair and retirement. The [device lifecycle](identity-lifecycle.md) defines their user-visible meaning, and the [security profile](security-profile.md#device-binding-record-schema) owns the referenced binding and proof formats.

> [!warn] Experimental protocol boundary
> This is a proposed application protocol, not an implemented authentication service. State signatures are exercised independently; persistent transition validation, freshness exchange and recovery must be integrated and tested before these records authorize interoperable MLS enrollment.

## How a device becomes trusted

An account has a stable identity and a roster of authorized devices. Each device has its own keys and permissions. A signed record can prove that a key signed something; the client must still establish that the account authorized that key for the requested operation.

For a first device, the client checks the account's initial identity and records the selected first-contact policy. This does not independently verify who owns an email address. For another device, an existing authorized administrator approves enrollment through the [pairing procedure](identity-lifecycle.md#pairing-another-device). Logging into the mailbox or syncing a password cannot supply that approval.

When the roster changes, clients retain the signed history and check the device against the accepted state before new work. An unrelated device addition can preserve an existing device's setup under the [continuity rules](provider-grants.md#continuity-and-freshness-have-different-scopes). A known revocation, conflicting branch or missing authority cannot be bypassed by replaying an older record. Reading retained correspondence is separate from permission to send, enroll a device or share history.

The current lifecycle below defines concurrent administrators, conflict repair and retirement. Its exact state1 rules explicitly inherit the shared record and proof rules in the following disclosures. Earlier state0 records retain their original meaning; they are not converted into state1 by changing a version field.

## Concurrent administration and retirement

Several devices can administer one account. Any device with the currently required administrative roles may initiate the corresponding operation; no designated primary device is required. Ordinary sending permission alone is insufficient. When their updates conflict, Email/2 keeps ordinary activity paused while those devices reconcile the account. A repair keeps every branch's revocations and requires approval from the administrators who remain authorized on every branch. No provider, timestamp or preferred device chooses a winner.

Renewing the same keys on two devices need not lose the account. The repair preserves only the permissions and validity shared by the conflicting bindings, then normal renewal can proceed from the repaired state. If nobody retains the necessary authority—for example, two administrators have revoked each other—the client explains that continuity cannot be repaired under this policy. It does not restore an old administrator through a password reset. Historical correspondence remains separate.

### Selected scope and encoding

The current Email/2 agreement selects identity-state1 and identity-bundle1 in addition to its retained state0 evidence. These are encodings within this one draft, not another product edition or a claim of deployed interoperability. State0 and bundle0 retain their exact historical rules. An old parser must hold an unsupported version; failure cannot fall back to another interpretation. New current-suite implementations must support this complete lifecycle before advertising it. The existing reference `IdentityStore` still implements state0 only; the repair component supplies candidate/proof evidence, not complete state1 acceptance.

State1 has exactly the state0 members plus `kind`: `{version,purpose,kind,principal,record,revision,previous,issuedAt,bindings,revoked,signer}`. Version is `"1"`, purpose `"identity-state"`; identifiers, scalar restrictions, binding references and counters retain their existing grammars. Kind is exactly `genesis`, `update`, `prune`, `repair` or `retire`. No field is optional or null. `previous` is an ASCII-sorted unique array of original state-payload SHA256 digests. A payload is at most1,048,576 octets and uses the common depth32/duplicate-name rules.

All state1 payload bytes MUST already equal their [RFC8785 canonical JSON](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785.html) representation. Verify that condition and the original bytes, rather than accepting an alternate encoding by reserialization. This schema uses only restricted ASCII strings, arrays and objects; counters remain strings, never JSON numbers. Binding payloads synthesized by a repair use the same canonical form. Other original state0/binding0 payloads retain their original-byte rules. The state digest remains SHA256 of original payload bytes, excluding all detached proofs.

Bundle1 has exactly `{version,purpose,principal,states,bindings}`, with version `"1"`, purpose `"identity-bundle"` and the existing principal/collection rules. Its complete strict JSON is at most4,194,304 octets; individual payload bounds remain independent. Bindings entries retain exactly `{payload,authorization,possession}` and binding0 cryptography. A state0 entry retains exactly `{payload,signature}`. A state1 entry has exactly `{payload,signature,approvals}`. Decode its payload's explicit version to choose that exact shape; unknown versions/extra fields reject it, without parser fallback. Payload is canonical padded base64 of original bytes. Signature is128 lowercase hexadecimal characters. Approvals is an ordered array of exact `{device,signature}` objects. Duplicated original state/binding digests, unresolved actor scope and malformed entries retain the existing whole-bundle rejection rules. A valid partial bundle is evidence, not a complete history or an installed roster.

State1's primary signature input is ASCII `email2/identity-state/v1`, NUL, U32 big-endian original payload length and those bytes. A non-primary repair approval uses the distinct domain `email2/identity-repair-approval/v1` with the same framing. Ordinary state1 kinds have empty approvals. The repair authorizer set and ordering below are derived independently; an unsigned list cannot supply it. All signatures use the existing Ed25519 rules.

### Genesis, ordinary updates and terminal retirement

Genesis has revision zero, no predecessor, one binding, no revocations and its sole device as signer. Apply the existing independently selected first-contact/self-binding/possession/time rules with the new signature domain. A genesis cannot replace a pin. An update has one predecessor, revision plus one without overflow, nondecreasing issuedAt, and the existing ordinary transition/delegation/revocation/admin-validity rules. Its changed binding0 records name that exact predecessor. A state1 update may upgrade an operational state0 head without changing principal or verification assurance. A state0 child of any state1 node is unsupported for new authority; retaining old evidence does not permit downgrade.

Retire has one operational predecessor, revision plus one, nondecreasing issuedAt, an empty bindings array, and revoked equal to the predecessor's revoked set union every predecessor device. Its signer must have current `enroll` and `revoke` authority in that predecessor. The originating client must retain an explicit account-retirement decision covering that exact principal and state before signing; loss of a subscription, inactivity, timer expiry or an ordinary device-removal request is not that decision. This is the only kind permitted to remove the final administrator and leave an empty roster. An ordinary update or prune must retain an administrator under the existing complete credible-time rule.

No successor of a validated retirement is permitted. Retain the terminal proof and revocation evidence, stop new application release and authority issuance for that identity, and invalidate live local grants/routes and pending work under their owning contracts. Do not erase readable history or presume remote deletion. Reopening requires the explicit new-identity path. A conflicting independently authorized branch learned later remains conflict evidence; it cannot make a retired path usable or allow a repair to invent an administrator for its empty roster. A retirement proposal from an unlinked or invalid source cannot suspend a pin.

### Validated history and the known frontier

Maintain proof-valid identity history separately from the one operational authorization result. A valid competing branch is not an operational head simply because its signatures validate. Every node must resolve through the selected pinned identity, complete parent and binding evidence, original signature/role policy and applicable historical/current checks. Retained accepted nodes keep their original acceptance evidence without regaining current powers. A previously unaccepted node cannot claim an old issuedAt to excuse expired predecessor signing authority: apply the owning version’s required predecessor-role/time checks for its new acceptance, or hold it as historical/unresolved evidence rather than operational authority. Cycles, unknown parents or exhausted disclosed traversal budgets hold validation. An incoming bundle cannot choose its own anchor, claim independent historical signing time, or prove that no state was withheld.

A known frontier is the sorted set of maximal proof-valid tips belonging to that pinned identity: no tip is an ancestor of another. Preserve all authorized competing paths and record collisions. A repair's parents must be an antichain and, at its installation boundary, cover exactly the locally known frontier. An incomplete, stale or redundant parent selection cannot clear conflict. Original record-ID collisions retain their evidence; a repair can reconcile the complete affected frontier without deleting that evidence or treating either colliding payload as an exact retry.

Fetch bounded missing public evidence through already selected channels before installation, then revalidate the complete known frontier under the local serialization boundary. Do not hold a database write lock during remote retrieval. A later valid branch absent from a repaired history reopens the affected conflict; no certificate proves global absence. A new repair must cover the new maximal frontier. Proof-valid descendants can be retained and checked while operational authority is paused, but only the restricted cleanup below or complete repair can advance reconciliation. They cannot release human content, authorize groups or issue ordinary grants while conflicted.

### One deterministic repair candidate

A repair has at least two parents. The parent records are original exact bytes, not reconstructed branch summaries. Let `P` be their sorted digests, and `S` be U32 big-endian parent count followed by each raw32-octet digest. Its revision is one plus the maximum parent revision; overflow holds repair. Its issuedAt is the maximum parent issuedAt, not a fresh signing-time or presence claim.

Its record is the first16 octets of SHA256 of ASCII `email2/identity-repair-record/v1`, NUL and `S`, encoded as lowercase hex. If those16 octets are all zero, use `00000000000000000000000000000001`. This finite identifier has no collision-free claim: retain conflicting original content and hold rather than overwrite. The complete payload digest remains the state identity.

Start revoked as the union of every parent's cumulative revoked set. Consider the union of devices in parent rosters, excluding that set. Resolve every original referenced binding through its independently validated branch:

1. If all references for a device name one exact binding digest, retain it. Absence on another branch is not prior authorization there: the complete repair certificate below explicitly reapproves that grant.
2. If references differ but all bindings have the same signing key, intersect their roles; set notBefore to the maximum original notBefore and notAfter to the minimum original notAfter. If roles are empty or the interval is disjoint, exclude and revoke the device. Otherwise derive a replacement binding0 with those exact roles sorted by ASCII, the same principal/device/key, and authority `{kind:"state",digest:P[0]}`. Its record is the first16 octets of SHA256 of ASCII `email2/identity-repair-binding/v1`, NUL, `S` and the ASCII device identifier, with the same zero replacement rule. Every other binding0 member has its existing exact meaning. This is a real fresh binding reference, not a claim that several hashes identify one old binding.
3. Conflicting signing keys exclude and revoke that device identifier. The repair cannot choose a key by arrival, timestamp, expiry or apparent device name. A separately approved fresh device identifier/binding is required afterward.

Sort the resulting binding references by device and revoked by device. A derived replacement requires the primary repair signer's binding-authorization signature and that exact device key's possession signature over its original derived bytes, using the existing binding0 domains. Its complete current interval must fit the derived validity. Retained original bindings retain their validated original proofs; the repair approvals are the additional cross-branch authorization. The [repair-specific binding validation procedure](security-profile.md#repair-bindings-stay-staged-until-the-certificate-is-accepted) keeps these proofs separate from ordinary enrollment and installs no binding before the complete certificate. A repair does not claim fresh entropy or compromise recovery merely because a same-key record changed.

### Who approves, and what can remain held

Derive the required authorizers as every device present on every parent, outside every parent revocation set, with the same signing key and both `enroll` and `revoke` roles in every referenced binding. Different binding digests do not exclude the healthy same-key renewal case. The set is structural: an expired, offline or objecting required device is not silently dropped. Each required device must be currently authorized under every parent throughout the complete credible interval at signing and final installation. Empty authorizer set, uncertainty or expired authority holds repair.

The payload signer is the ASCII-first required authorizer. Its ordinary state1 signature and exactly one repair-approval proof from every other required authorizer, sorted by device, form the certificate. This first proof signer is not an account primary and cannot approve alone. All approvals cover the exact deterministic payload. Duplicate, extra, missing, foreign-role or different-domain proofs cannot supply a certificate. Valid signature variants over identical bytes are equivalent proof evidence, not another state.

Every authorizer must be able to authorize the complete resulting change against **each** parent. In addition to `enroll`/`revoke`, every role newly granted relative to that parent must occur in that authorizer's parent binding. Branch-only enrollments therefore require explicit cross-branch approval; a revoked ancestor's signature is not new approval. All required authorizers remain authorized in the repaired roster, and at least one has current enroll/revoke powers throughout the installation interval. The original permissions and shorter binding lifetimes cannot be widened by calling the operation a repair.

Before signing, a client validates the whole candidate, all original and derived binding proofs and its exact known frontier, and retains a local decision covering the actual device grants, removals, role changes and validity narrowing. An explicitly applicable remembered policy can cover that decision; a network request or signature from another device cannot manufacture it. Refusing the one candidate holds it rather than authorizing a different winner for the same frontier. Deterministic bytes prevent honest devices signing different repair choices for that identical frontier; no timer-based vote release or improvised abort certificate is selected.

Prune provides separately approved revocation-only cleanup. It has exactly one proof-valid frontier-tip predecessor, the next revision and nondecreasing issuedAt. The signer must have current revoke power in that parent. It must remove at least one device and add exactly the removed identifiers to cumulative revoked; it cannot add a binding, replace a key, change any surviving binding or grant a role. An unchanged-roster continuity checkpoint uses an ordinary update and requires enrollment authority; a revoke-only signer cannot issue it as an empty prune. Retain at least one currently valid administrator. It is permitted while the overall identity is conflicted solely to construct a new reconciliation frontier; it grants no operational authority. Removing an unavailable or compromised required authorizer requires that explicit authorized removal on every relevant branch, not a timeout. A later opposing revocation still remains evidence and may leave no surviving authority.

No same-identity override is selected when revocation or expiry leaves no qualifying administration. The client preserves history and conflict evidence and offers the explicit new-identity path. A physical key, old archive, synced password, provider login or billing status cannot supply the missing authority.

A retiring client prepares any approved terminal notice before committing retirement and retiring unneeded signing secrets. The [terminal-notice procedure](#deliver-a-retirement-proof-without-reopening-the-account) permits delivery of that frozen proof afterward without restoring operational authority. Retained notice bytes do not keep a withdrawn route or grant alive. A terminal proof is never permission to sign new operational work.

### Proof circulation and exact retries

One device can collect proofs from several other devices and pass them on together. The device assembling that share does not become the signer of those proofs. Each approval still belongs to the device that made it, and receiving a share does not approve or install the repair.

A repair share is strict JSON with exactly `{version,purpose,principal,parents,candidate,by,proof,bindings}`. Version is `"1"`; purpose `"identity-repair-share"`; principal is the pinned identity; parents is a partial identity-bundle1 supplying the candidate's original dependencies; candidate is canonical base64 of the exact deterministic repair payload; by is the existing device identifier; proof is empty or one128-hex state1/repair-approval signature, using the primary or non-primary domain according to the derived authorizer set. A nonempty proof requires that authorizer and all the checks above. Empty supplies no state approval.

Bindings is an array of exact `{payload,authorization,possession}` entries for a subset of the candidate's derived bindings, sorted by original derived payload digest with no duplicates. Each proof is empty or one128-hex signature; at least one is nonempty. Empty is explicitly incomplete, never valid authority. Verify nonempty proofs independently against the one exact derived payload and its independently selected keys. The primary can supply authorization and the affected device can supply possession; only a complete pair can enter a final bundle. No private key or checkpoint travels in a share. A non-authorizer can supply its own possession proof without acquiring an approval vote.

The `by` member identifies the assembling device. A nonempty top-level `proof` must be that device's own state signature or repair approval, using its derived role and signature domain. Binding proofs may have been collected from other devices: verify authorization under the derived primary authorizer's key and possession under the affected device's key. Do not substitute the assembler's key or attribute a forwarded proof to it. A public file's `by` value does not authenticate its assembler. In a sealed identity-admin share, `by` must match the authenticated source device, which must independently satisfy the administrative proof-circulation permission below. Forwarding a valid proof grants no additional vote, role or sending permission.

Keep signature verification separate from present authority. An authentic retained proof can remain inspectable when its original authority interval or current applicability is unresolved. Label that result explicitly; it cannot count as a currently usable approval, install a roster or trigger a response. A claimed `issuedAt` does not resolve the missing time evidence. Likewise, the embedded `parents` bundle supplies candidate evidence, not an automatically accepted history or a new identity pin.

Shares obey the common depth32/duplicate-name rules and a4,194,304-octet total bound; the candidate retains its own1,048,576-octet bound. Missing dependencies may arrive through other valid bounded bundles. Validate strict framing before staging any proof, and merge only authentic proof evidence for the same candidate and exact device/purpose. More evidence cannot change the candidate. Retain exact emitted bytes and retry identity before handoff. Changed candidate bytes require another complete derivation/frontier; a transport retry cannot rewrite them.

Public bundles and shares may use a trusted local file exchange or a separately authorized configured public-evidence directory. Their proposed logical types are `application/vnd.email2.identity-bundle+json` and `application/vnd.email2.identity-repair-share+json`, without parameters. An explicitly selected public-evidence file exchange may carry that file as one direct canonical MIME-base64 attachment after a generic notice in outer multipart/mixed; a filename is not authority. This deliberate public export is not the automatic current-suite control transport and cannot be inferred from a failed private exchange. Do not inspect forwarded/nested copies for automatic activation. These objects expose device/authority relationships even without personal mailbox fields; disclosure and account routing require their independent local policies. Their contents never select an HTTP callback, service login, recipient mailbox or arbitrary retrieval destination.

Automatic current-suite administrative mail MUST use the sealed-control layer, with no readable proof copy. It adds kind `identity-admin`, whose body is exactly one bundle1 or repair-share1 selected by its strict version/purpose. Its outer signature and recipient/key/deadline binding remain mandatory. The owning administrative validator, rather than ordinary message-send permission, checks this kind: the source must match a valid relevant branch authorizer, or the exact device supplying its own derived possession proof, within the selected pinned identity and current branch-scoped roles. This narrow proof-circulation permission can operate during that identity's conflict; it cannot authorize application content, operational enrollment, provider grants or MLS state. New identities cannot install their own pin through it. The recipient is independently selected, and owns the exact still-eligible control key. Existing HPKE publication/receiving limits, original key custody, transport permissions and relay masking remain effective. No conflict exception repairs a revoked route or grants an account credential. If the selected carriage cannot preserve required masking/confidentiality, hold that route and offer another independently permitted exchange; never send a readable replacement automatically. The existing inline identity-bundle size limit still applies; larger proof histories require separate bounded records, not a larger implicit allowance.

### Deliver a retirement proof without reopening the account

Retiring an identity does not depend on every correspondent being online. A client can prepare confidential notices while its administrator is still authorized, commit retirement locally, then deliver the retained notices when a permitted transport is available. It does not keep signing keys alive for that job or wait for acknowledgements before retiring.

A retirement notice contains only the terminal state and the evidence needed to validate it. It is not a final chat message, permission to keep a relay route open or a request for a reply. If required privacy or transport permissions cannot be preserved, the notice stays held. A separately authorized public-evidence export remains a deliberate disclosure, never an automatic fallback.

::: {.details summary="Frozen terminal notices, sender validation and receipt"}

This is the terminal-only case of identity-admin/sealed-control-v0; it adds no wire field, signature domain, media type or transport grant.

1. **Prepare under live authority.** While the retirement predecessor is still the independently accepted operational head, validate its exact retirement state/certificate and all required original dependencies. Retain the explicit retirement decision and independently selected notice recipients/disclosure scope. Each confidential body is one strict bundle1 containing that exact retirement and only its required original ancestor/binding evidence. It contains no repair share, unrelated branch, application body, request or new authorization. Resolve each recipient's normal independently eligible control-key publication, association and required masking. The sender is exactly the retirement state's signer, with its predecessor state and binding in the sealed intent; verify its original enroll/revoke authority and all normal creation/key/intent deadlines before signing and encryption.
2. **Commit before release.** Freeze each exact signed intent, ciphertext, recipient/key, original deadlines and dependencies in the retirement transaction. Atomically commit the terminal acceptance, ordinary-authority invalidation, retirement/secret-cleanup obligations and the retained notices' terminal-only release permission. No notice may leave before that transaction commits. A failed transaction cannot leave a releasable terminal notice; a completed transaction cannot be undone by cancelling its notification. Crash recovery distinguishes prepared bytes from the committed terminal decision and never recreates a notice by restoring retired keys.
3. **Deliver only retained bytes.** After retirement, this narrow permission replaces only the normal requirement that the notice's sender remain operational. Recheck the committed exact terminal/ciphertext identity, intended recipient/key/association, original release interval, local disclosure/masking policy and separately valid transport permission at the ordinary final release boundary. Respect new recipient revocation, key withdrawal, local notice cancellation and newly learned conflict evidence. Such a change holds the affected notice; it does not revive the sender, re-encrypt under another recipient key, extend the deadline or produce a new signed intent. Exact retries retain their original identities and uncertain outcomes. A lost result is not permission to issue another notice.
4. **Keep transport independent.** Provider mailbox submission may remain separately authorized after an endpoint identity retires. It can carry the frozen notice only if the actual account/transport contract permits that operation. An identity-derived link, grant or relay disabled by retirement remains disabled; no retained delivery proof or ciphertext overrides it. An unavailable required masked route keeps the notice held. A configured public-evidence channel may distribute the already public terminal proof under its own account/disclosure authority, without claiming an operational endpoint or a confidential mail fallback. No new provider endpoint or generic queue is implied.
5. **Receive as terminal evidence.** Select this exception only after complete HPKE authentication and strict kind/body/recipient validation. Independently resolve the already pinned principal and exact original predecessor/retirement evidence. Verify the retirement proof, its original authorizer, and the sealed signature using that exact predecessor binding; require the intent's sender/device/state/binding to match those records. Retain the original creation bounds, receiving-key promise, applicable clock/role checks and replay/conflict rules. The sole exception is that this validated terminal state itself need not leave its signer operational. It cannot excuse an unrelated known revocation, unresolved competing authority or an expired predecessor by trusting an old claimed signing time. Without sufficient evidence, stage the notice as pending/conflicted rather than applying it.
6. **Install no intermediate authority.** Validate missing ancestors as proof dependencies without exposing a transient operational head, installing advertisements or enabling any included device. Commit accepted terminal evidence and its invalidations atomically under the ordinary state1 acceptance rules, or retain incomplete/conflicting evidence without those effects. An exact accepted retirement retry returns its retained result; different authenticated bytes retain their original conflict meaning. A retirement on one branch cannot silently erase another branch. No receipt, automatic response, new mailbox association, history transfer or application send follows from this notice.

The receiver validates the exact proof and its limited purpose; it cannot cryptographically prove the sender's local commit order or signing time. A journal claim is not remote attestation. For an already accepted retirement, later theft of its old signing key cannot turn this notice into a new operational capability: this path accepts only the independently valid terminal proof and its dependencies. This is not protection against a compromised administrator creating a retirement that the receiver cannot yet distinguish from an authorized one. Apply the existing revocation, conflict, time and administration rules; unsupported or insufficient evidence remains invalid/pending. This does not promise immediate global retirement observation or recall of earlier deliveries.

:::

### Select a confidential repair recipient

A conflict pauses ordinary identity use, but it must not make the narrowly authorized repair-proof exchange depend on completing that same repair first. This exception applies only to a validated identity-admin bundle/share and its independently selected recipient. It grants no ordinary receiving advertisement, application sending, enrollment, history, provider or route permission.

For a recipient with a conflict-free accepted identity, keep the normal control-key selection. For a recipient in the same independently pinned conflicted identity being repaired, resolve the complete locally known proof-valid frontier before selecting its existing published control key:

1. Validate the original signed publication, exact publisher principal/device/key, original binding and accepted anchor using retained original evidence. The publication is evidence for that control key only; its application/package fields do not become live support or admission material through this exception. No incoming share can choose a new pin, key or recipient.
2. Require the recipient device on **every** frontier tip, with the same signing key and current receive authority throughout the complete credible interval. Any known branch revocation, retirement, conflicting key, missing path or absent device holds this confidential destination. A device present on only one branch does not obtain automatic confidential access by requesting a possession proof.
3. From the publication's accepted anchor through every relevant accepted path to those tips, require continuous authorization for that same device/key/receive role, with no revocation or validity gap. Same-key binding renewals can retain only this limited control-key eligibility when validity intervals overlap or touch; a changed binding cannot widen the original key's lifetime or its privacy scope. Do not choose one convenient branch or skip a prior repair boundary. A publication from an unrelated or unaccepted branch is insufficient.
4. Keep the original key's publication and receiving windows and actual protected private-key custody. New encryption still needs its original publication window, intent/body deadlines and current branch-scoped sender authority; receiving-only retention cannot authorize a newly prepared proof. Recheck the known frontier, key and local approval/disclosure policy at final release under the local serialization boundary. A later branch can invalidate the earlier selection.
5. Validate the complete narrow administrative body and its actual sender proof under the proof-circulation rules before encryption. Retain exact ciphertext/replay identity and independently valid account/transport/relay permission. The exception cannot refresh a withdrawn relay, invent a missing credential or disclose a different body through an administrative label.

If no eligible key remains, show confidential proof delivery as unavailable. An independently permitted existing channel or deliberate public-evidence export retains its own disclosure rules; do not send a readable fallback automatically. Administrative-only devices without receive authority or a usable publication do not acquire it through this procedure. Their required signatures/possession proofs may still be collected through independently authorized local exchange; the repair cannot silently drop them.

The same scope applies on receipt: decrypting a capsule and checking branch-scoped administrative authority stages only its independently valid proof evidence. It does not resolve the identity conflict, install a roster or activate any other body kind. Final repair installation still requires the complete deterministic certificate and current frontier. The ordinary cached-assertion unchanged-binding rule remains unchanged for all nonadministrative uses.

Receiving a share does not request an automatic acknowledgement or establish approval. Only a preexisting locally enabled repair of an independently validated own-account conflict can cause production of a new approved proof. Each local proof is retained before sending and emitted at most once to each independently selected configured repair recipient; exact retransmission follows the existing finite retry/uncertainty policy. Duplicate input does not automatically resend. Possession signing first verifies the exact derived own-key binding and its narrower scope. No incoming address, unsigned readiness flag or public message can trigger enrollment, key export or an unbounded proof-response loop.

### Installation, later branches and dependent authority

**Check an older certificate against its own parents.** To validate retained or newly received repair evidence, resolve the exact original parent digests named by that certificate. Require at least two distinct proof-valid parents forming an antichain under the same independently selected anchor, and derive the candidate, required authorizers and binding changes from those parents. Compare the original candidate bytes and check every required state, approval, binding-authorization and possession proof. Do not substitute today's frontier for the signed parent set. A later branch does not by itself make an otherwise valid historical certificate malformed or its signatures invalid.

This historical check establishes only the evidence supported by those proofs. It does not establish when signatures were made, supply missing predecessor-role or time evidence, prove that the parents were ever the complete locally known frontier, or authorize installation. Apply the [history-validation rules](#validated-history-and-the-known-frontier); a claimed old issuedAt cannot bypass a required current-authority check for previously unaccepted evidence. Missing dependencies or authority evidence remain unresolved. Retain independently valid evidence without replacing an operational head or dropping other branches, revocations or collisions.

A later independently authorized unequal payload under the same typed record identifier remains collision evidence. Report that collision separately from the older certificate's exact-parent derivation and signature results; do not overwrite either original or describe a valid signature as invalid because the collision arrived later. Historical inspection cannot clear the collision hold. Preparing a new candidate still applies the deterministic output-ID collision refusal, without a random replacement or winner choice.

**Apply a repair only against the current frontier.** Candidate creation and signing use the complete known frontier. Historical certificate validation and current installation must be separate outcomes: a certificate can remain valid evidence while installation is held. No successful historical check may set an installed or current-authority flag by itself.

After complete proof collection, rederive the exact candidate against the current known frontier, recheck current parent-scoped authorizers/roles/time and every derived possession proof, and compare that frontier atomically. Persist the complete original certificate, binding evidence, covered parent graph, retained collisions/revocations, new head and dependency invalidation together. A crash commits none or all of this logical acceptance. Only then expose the repaired roster. This is not physical-erasure or whole-storage rollback protection; replay/head evidence must be protected under the existing storage/freshness contract.

An exact accepted certificate retry returns its retained result without reinstalling or extending authority. Different valid proof bytes for the same payload do not fork state. An authenticated state with different payload remains independent evidence; invalid signatures and unknown claims cannot poison an established head. Installation never drops a known competing tip, chooses a higher revision, or hides a newly learned revocation. Reconciliation at an old frontier cannot overwrite a newer local state. Later missing branches reopen conflict and require a new complete frontier; already disclosed content is not recalled.

**Acceptance cases.** Receive the same complete certificate before and after an additional independently authorized branch, and in both arrival orders. Its exact-parent proof result must agree when the same required validation evidence is available; its installation result must reflect the actual current frontier and authority. Also exercise a missing named parent, a redundant ancestor parent, a certificate with a valid alternate signature over identical payload bytes, and previously unaccepted expired authorization without independent historical evidence. None may be promoted to current authority merely because the payload claims an old time. A later branch must remain in history when an older certificate is retained, and an exact installed-certificate retry must not reinstall it over that branch.

Repair is an explicit **fresh-authorization boundary**. Earlier cached discovery assertions, provider links/grants, route permissions, unfinished pairing approvals, pending freshness results and setup-configuration transfers cannot cross it using the single-parent unchanged-binding shortcut—even for a retained signing key. Preserve their original evidence and uncertain submission identities; acquire appropriately scoped fresh authorizations/support from the repaired head under the existing account and remembered policies. Do not rotate an account incarnation or claim a human identity change merely because a valid repair retained the same pinned principal. Independent verification retains its original scope; it does not become stronger.

Installed MLS state and retained historical receipts are not restored or rewritten by identity repair. Recheck current keys/roles and actual original membership/context under their owning contracts before new effects. A same-key retained member may remain eligible where that contract permits it; changed keys, binding-dependent grants or unresolved membership require their actual transition or fresh admission. No merged identity automatically installs a new MLS member, copies history or replays an answer. A pre-repair sealed intent may remain historical evidence, but live use across this boundary requires the owning new authorization rather than guessing a path through the DAG. Already decoded readable history remains available and archive import remains inert.

An implementation must not claim this lifecycle from the first-repair derivation component. Acceptance needs full v1 schema/proof and parent-graph validation; ordinary upgrades, cleanup, deterministic repairs and retirement; complete/partial shares and possession; honest simultaneous renewals and enrollment; stale/unknown/hidden branches, role/expiry/key conflicts and cumulative revocation; concurrent installation/restart; invalid proof non-poisoning; required-signer unavailability; expired-administration outcomes; protected carriage and no response loops; and every dependent fresh-authorization boundary through actual account/discovery/route/MLS release. Independent implementations, cryptographic review and production participation remain unproven external evidence.

::::

## Identity proof bundle

::: {.details summary="State0 bundle schema and evidence limits"}

The version-0 exchange object is strict UTF-8 JSON under the event encoding rules, with exactly five members: version (`0`), purpose (`identity-bundle`), principal (an enp0 account identifier), states (an array) and bindings (an array). At least one array is nonempty. Every enclosed record must name that principal. Array order has no authority or processing-order meaning.

Each states entry has exactly payload and signature. Each bindings entry has exactly payload, authorization and possession. Payload is the original record bytes encoded with the standard base64 alphabet, required padding and zero unused pad bits, without whitespace. Re-encoding decoded bytes must reproduce that string exactly. Proofs are 128 lowercase hexadecimal characters representing the Ed25519 signatures defined by their respective purpose. A state's payload must identify version0 and purpose identity-state; a binding's must identify version0 and purpose device-binding. Full record-schema validation remains mandatory after strict decoding.

The complete decoded bundle is limited to1048576 octets and the event encoding's nesting bound. This includes the base64 strings and proof overhead; it is not a separate allowance for every entry. Local allocation limits may cause incomplete processing under the existing resource-limit rules. Each embedded payload is also independently subject to strict UTF-8, duplicate-name, scalar-value and nesting checks. Duplicate exact payload digests within either collection make that bundle invalid. No partial effects are permitted on malformed bundles.

The wrapper is not signed as a unit. Signatures and references cover the enclosed original record bytes, so whitespace or member-order changes in the wrapper preserve those proofs. The principal field is an indexing hint checked against every payload, never standalone authority. Removing a record can make validation pending; it cannot make a required proof optional. Adding a record cannot grant a role without its complete chain and applicable policy. Unknown members are rejected rather than treated as unsigned extensions with hidden security meaning.

Bundles may contain a partial history or records in arbitrary dependency order. Receivers resolve references by exact payload digest across supplied and previously stored evidence. They must not infer that the bundle is a complete roster, the newest state or proof of absence of revocation. A first-contact genesis claim needs its state and binding, all three proofs, configured policy and applicable time checks. Neither an empty collection nor a missing predecessor requests deletion of stored state.

This selects the application carrier. MIME media-type registration, its ordinary-mail packaging and discovery-service request/response binding remain required integration work; the bundle's purpose string is not a registered content type or an instruction to hide mail in legacy clients. These records contain public keys and authorization evidence, never private keys or historical message secrets. They can still expose device relationships and must follow the discovery privacy policy.

:::

## Exact state payload

::: {.details summary="State0 payload fields and revision rules"}

A state payload is strict UTF-8 JSON under the [event encoding rules](event-encoding.md). It has exactly the following members. No member is optional, and null is not a value.

| Member | Type and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| version | String `0` |
| purpose | String `identity-state` |
| principal | Account identifier with the enp0 encoding |
| record | Nonzero ObjectId, stable for retries of these exact bytes |
| revision | Canonical unsigned-64 decimal string |
| previous | Empty array for genesis; otherwise an array containing exactly one lowercase SHA256 digest |
| issuedAt | Canonical unsigned-64 decimal string of UTC epoch seconds |
| bindings | Nonempty array of binding references as defined below |
| revoked | Array of distinct device identifiers, sorted by exact ASCII spelling |
| signer | Device identifier of the state authorizer |

A binding reference is exactly an object with device and digest members. Device uses the end0 encoding; digest is the lowercase hexadecimal SHA256 of the exact referenced binding payload bytes. Bindings are sorted by device identifier and contain no repeated device. A device cannot occur in both bindings and revoked. Sorted arrays make equivalent rosters easy to compare; they do not canonicalize the payload or change the exact-byte signature rule.

The state digest is SHA256 over the original payload bytes, excluding its detached proof. References and signatures MUST NOT be computed from a reconstructed JSON object. A proof wrapper must retain the exact payload. Unknown versions, members and enum values cannot be silently ignored.

Revision zero is genesis and requires an empty previous array and an empty revoked array. Every other revision requires one previous digest. Revision is a chain counter, not a timestamp, voting weight or preference among competing branches. A successor's revision MUST equal its predecessor's revision plus one, without unsigned overflow. issuedAt MUST NOT precede its predecessor's issuedAt; a later timestamp never resolves a fork or supplies a trusted clock.

:::

## State proof

::: {.details summary="State0 signature framing"}

The detached state signature uses Ed25519 and the ASCII domain `email-next/identity-state/v0` followed by one zero octet, the four-octet unsigned big-endian payload length and the original payload bytes. The experimental payload bound is 1048576 octets. Keys and signatures have the lowercase hexadecimal encodings selected for device-binding proofs. This signature domain is distinct from binding authorization, device possession and MLS signatures.

For genesis, verify the proof under its sole binding's signing key after validating that binding's self-binding and possession proofs. For a successor, resolve signer exclusively in the predecessor's accepted bindings, then verify under that binding's key and authority. A newly proposed key MUST NOT authorize its own insertion by appearing as signer in the successor. If a key changes during a transition, the state proof still uses the predecessor's authorized key.

:::

## Genesis and first contact

::: {.details summary="Initial identity acceptance and policy"}

Genesis has exactly one binding reference, whose device equals signer. The referenced binding must name the same principal, carry first-contact authority and pass both proof checks. Its roles must include enroll and revoke so the identity can administer its initial roster. The historical role grammar includes `recover`; its presence does not select an emergency recovery operation in this suite and never creates a provider recovery right.

For initial use, distinguish an interval wholly after binding expiry (`not-current`, expired), wholly before validity (`not-current`, not-yet-valid), and an uncertain interval crossing either boundary (pending). A proof can remain cryptographically valid in all three cases. None permits initial pinning or restores a revoked role. Re-observing expired material must not erase a previously recorded pin or historical evidence; using that pin for new operations still requires current validity and policy checks.

Acceptance of a genesis record follows first-contact policy and remains distinct from verified ownership of a mailbox. Genesis cannot replace an established principal or add a device to it. When no genesis has been accepted and multiple self-consistent claims are available, first contact is ambiguous: arrival order or a later issuedAt does not establish ownership. Once a genesis is pinned under the configured first-contact policy, another self-signed genesis is an unlinked claim. Preserve it as untrusted competing evidence, but do not replace or suspend the pin merely because the claim names the same public principal. Anyone can copy that identifier. This differs from two conflicting successors whose authorization both traces to the accepted identity. A permissive first-contact pin remains vulnerable to substitution before it is established; this rule does not upgrade it to verified identity.

:::

## Ordinary transition

::: {.details summary="State0 delegation and roster transitions"}

A receiver resolves and validates the predecessor before considering the proposed state. A missing predecessor leaves the transition pending. A rejected predecessor cannot authorize a successor. The predecessor must belong to the same principal and satisfy the revision relationship. Each referenced binding must resolve to a valid payload for the named device and principal; a digest match alone establishes none of its authorization.

Compare the proposed roster with the predecessor:

| Change | Required predecessor authority |
| --- | --- |
| Add a device binding | Signer has enroll |
| Replace a binding, including validity, key or role changes | Signer has enroll; an authorized device replacement follows lifecycle checks |
| Remove a device | Signer has revoke |
| Add or replace and remove in one transition | Signer has both enroll and revoke |
| Change administrative roles | Signer has enroll and every role being granted |

For an ordinary transition, every role in each newly added or replaced binding must occur in the signer’s predecessor binding. This includes roles retained by a renewal: issuing a replacement is a new grant, not reuse of the superseded authorization. Unchanged exact binding references retain their existing authorization. There is no implicit administrative superuser. All newly added or replaced bindings must name this exact predecessor digest as their state authority, carry a binding authorization proof made by this state signer, and carry the proposed device's possession proof. Unchanged references retain their original authorization evidence; they need not be reissued for each successor. For a changed signing key under a still-present device identifier, the possession proof is made by the proposed new key, and binding authorization and the state proof are made by the predecessor-authorized state signer, which may be another administrator. No separate signature by the superseded target key is required. Acceptance supersedes that old binding for new work; pending deliveries and live groups still follow their independent lifecycle and membership checks. A previously revoked identifier cannot use this replacement path.

Revoked is cumulative. Every removed device must be added, previous entries must remain, and no entry may be deleted. A new revocation entry must identify a device removed from the predecessor roster; this configuration does not preemptively revoke unknown device identifiers. An identifier in this set cannot be re-enrolled; a returning device obtains a fresh device identifier and an authorized new binding. Revocation does not erase the retained binding or historical attribution. A key or role replacement for a still-present device does not by itself add its identifier to revoked; the old binding becomes superseded for future operations when the transition is accepted.

The proposed roster must retain at least one device with enroll and revoke roles in the same valid binding. This historical state0 configuration does not define an account-retirement transition; the selected [state1 lifecycle](#concurrent-administration-and-retirement) supplies the explicit terminal operation. A deliberate loss of all administration is not expressible as a misleading usable state; it requires the selected state1 retirement operation or an explicit new-identity procedure. Administrative validity is checked at transition acceptance time, with uncertain time holding the operation. If time is represented as an uncertainty interval, the signer, each newly granted binding and at least one retained administrator must each be valid throughout that interval; different administrators valid only at opposite endpoints do not satisfy this requirement. These rules do not extend an expired binding.

A signed state with an unchanged roster and revoked set is allowed as a continuity checkpoint. It still requires an enroll-authorized signer, the exact predecessor, the next revision and ordinary proof validation. Such a checkpoint proves only that an authorized key signed that state. It does not prove that the receiver has learned every later transition.

:::

## Persistence, replays and branches

::: {.details summary="Durable acceptance, replay and conflict rules"}

Persist the accepted payload, its exact digest, binding evidence, proof and predecessor relationship before exposing newly granted authority. Installation must compare the predecessor against the durable current head atomically. If another successor won that comparison, retain the candidate for branch evaluation instead of applying it over the new head.

Exact-byte retries return the stored result. The state-record collision key is the anchored principal plus record identifier, shared across state versions and branches of that authority history. Different authorized original payload bytes under that key are a conflict, even if the payloads claim different signers or devices. State records and device-binding records have separate typed collision indexes; reusing a spelling across those types does not equate their payloads. An unlinked self-signed claim does not acquire authority to create such a conflict by copying the identifier. Retain authenticated record collisions even when all known records lie on one ancestry path and the known frontier has one maximal tip. That is not the multi-parent frontier required by the selected repair operation: do not invent a one-parent repair, discard the collided ancestor or resume authority from the newest-looking record. Affected operational use stays held. If continuity cannot be repaired under the selected rules, the deliberate new-identity procedure remains available without transferring the old pin, verification or history authority. A historical valid state can support historical verification but cannot roll back the accepted head, remove revocation evidence or reauthorize a superseded binding. Backup restoration follows the same rule.

Two independently valid successors of one predecessor constitute a branch conflict. Arrival order, revision, timestamp, provider ranking and number of relays MUST NOT select a winner. Hold new operations whose authority depends on the competing states and retain both paths. Already disclosed messages cannot be recalled; historical reading remains available.

> [!ex] Two authorized devices update at once
> Alice's laptop and phone can each prepare a valid device addition from the same account state. Receiving both creates a conflict; a larger revision or an earlier arrival cannot settle it. Concurrent binding renewals can also conflict even when both devices keep the same keys. The state0 experiment holds affected operations. The selected state1 lifecycle defines repair when qualifying authority survives, and an explicit new-identity outcome when it does not.

State0 does not encode a multi-parent merge or authorize automatic conflict recovery. The selected [state1 lifecycle](#concurrent-administration-and-retirement) defines its exact repair instead. State1 requires the complete certificate and revocation-preserving algorithm below; a signature-valid candidate alone cannot claim repaired continuity. An implementation lacking that full lifecycle holds the conflict; deliberately creating and verifying a new identity is a continuity break, not a repaired chain. The client must not present this exceptional outcome as routine device enrollment.

:::

## Freshness and recovery boundary

::: {.details summary="Freshness limits and administrative loss"}

A state can be cryptographically valid and stale. The state format cannot prove absence of a later revocation, and issuedAt is not such proof. The [freshness contract](freshness.md) selects cached continuity for ordinary asynchronous use, defines explicit recent-checkpoint age of the current state's claimed issuance time, and binds live confirmation to the original authorized device challenge. Cached continuity has no independent proof of unseen remote changes; signed binding and capability validity still limit use. A recent declared issuance time is not trusted signing time or live presence, and fresh device evidence cannot override a known removal. The selected age, whole credible clock interval and held unavailable outcome apply at release. A cached state alone MUST NOT support an instantaneous-global-revocation claim. No plaintext fallback is implied by unresolved freshness.

The recover role records a separately granted capability; it does not grant an exception to ordinary transition rules in this configuration. The initial suite does not select an emergency override. When no usable enrollment authority remains, follow the [administrative-loss procedure](identity-lifecycle.md#when-administrative-devices-are-unavailable): unavailable approval waits, while deliberate abandonment of lost authority requires an explicit new identity. A surviving send/receive-only device cannot promote itself. [Archive recovery](encrypted-archives.md) can restore historical reading without changing that identity outcome. A recovery construction needs its own authenticated operation, state-transfer channel, replay handling and history-access policy. Mailbox login, password reset, a directory response or possession of an old backup is insufficient. Existing authorized devices can perform ordinary enroll/revoke operations when their bindings permit them; that is different from using an emergency recovery authority.

:::

## Current device authorization

An operational use-time authorization check resolves the device from the locally accepted head, not from an incoming binding or a caller-selected historical digest. The caller supplies the authenticated credential identity and signature key, the operation (`send` or `receive`), the current time uncertainty interval and the current trust policy. A receiver MUST hold a missing identity or unresolved accepted conflict. An unverified pin and its successors cannot satisfy a policy requiring independent verification.

The selected device must remain in the accepted roster and outside its cumulative revoked set. Its exact binding digest must resolve to retained accepted evidence. The authenticated leaf signing key must match that binding, the operation must be among its roles, and the binding must be valid throughout the time interval. A wholly expired or not-yet-valid interval is not current; a crossing or unavailable interval is pending. Successful checks return the accepted head and binding digests as dependencies of the decision.

This result is a snapshot, not a reusable delivery permission. The [transport release boundary](transport.md#local-authorization-and-release) must recheck and serialize those dependencies before release, together with the [group requirements](group-conversations.md#applying-account-changes-to-an-mls-group). A newer local head or conflict invalidates a prior decision; stronger remote-freshness policy remains separately applicable. Historical bindings remain evidence but cannot substitute for the current head.

## Conformance evidence

The [experiment reference](evidence/README.md) records executable checks for this chapter. The following layers compose into the local identity pipeline; passing one layer does not establish the prerequisites of another.

| Layer | Evidence | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payload schema | Exact fields, counters, identifiers and roster structure | No proof or authority established by syntax |
| Detached proofs | Actual Ed25519 signatures and separation of binding, possession and state purposes | No mailbox ownership or freshness established |
| Identity bundle | Strict decoding, exact payload references, partial dependencies and scope checks | No claim that the received collection is complete or current |
| First-contact verification | Genesis relationship, three proofs, roles and time interval | Successful claim remains unverified |
| First-contact storage | Retry/reopen, explicit policy, ambiguity and unlinked-claim handling | No independent identity verification or mailbox mapping |
| Successor verification | Trusted predecessor anchor, changed-binding proofs and role policy | Result remains uncommitted until the database transaction succeeds |
| Successor storage | Atomic head updates, retained authorized forks, revocation replay resistance, record collisions and process exit before commit | No distributed freshness, branch repair, backup rollback protection or outgoing MLS coordination |
| Current device authorization | Snapshot of accepted head, roster, key, operation role, validity and trust policy; revocation persists through reopen and replay | Decision is not atomically coupled to transport release |

The [multiple-administrator counterexamples](evidence/README.md#multiple-administrator-concurrency-counterexamples) additionally exercise actual signed concurrent additions, same-key renewals, mutual revocations and a later descendant. They demonstrate the current conflict boundary, not a completed repair. A separate [deterministic repair candidate](evidence/README.md#deterministic-administrator-repair-candidate) exercises common-authority approval and restrictive same-key renewal without installing a state. The selected state1 contract defines repair, retirement and downstream authorization; their full joined implementation remains absent. The candidate component does not remove the state0 store’s hold.

The [successor storage tests](evidence/README.md#durable-successor-acceptance) exercise separate concurrent database connections and an actual child-process exit after writes but before commit. The latter demonstrates rollback and successful retry after reopening, not resilience to power loss, disk corruption or restored historical snapshots.

An unverified first-contact pin remains unverified through authorized transitions. A stricter current verification policy still applies. Unlinked genesis claims cannot suspend the pin; authorized successor conflicts hold affected operations. These experiments implement local evidence handling, not a complete authentication service or independently interoperable MLS client. A schema-valid result MUST NOT be exposed as an accepted identity state, and a locally accepted state MUST NOT be presented as globally current merely because its signatures verify.
